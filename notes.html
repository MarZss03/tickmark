    The money is in the bank. The loan still shows a balance.

    The problem

    A payout wire reaches the bank on the closing date. The loan system applies it three days later, so the loan shows interest still owing. Is that the borrower's shortfall, or our delay?

    Finance operations staff meet this all day: two records that should agree, and don't. Before anything can move, someone has to work out which kind of gap it is.

    • An entry error.Someone keyed 6,580.00 for 6,850.00, or imported the same timesheet twice.
    • A timing or sync gap.The money arrived; a hold or a batch has not caught up.
    • A real business difference.The wire really was late, or the closing date really moved.

    The facts that decide it usually sit with other teams. One team knows when the money arrived; another system records when it reached the loan; a third person is allowed to say which date applies. The person moving the case has to find each piece, ask the right person, and keep the answers straight. If one input is wrong, anything reasoning from it, a person or a model, carries the error forward.

    What I found

    From my own work

    Mortgage servicing and payouts

    I work in mortgage servicing and payouts at a Canadian bank, and before that I was a real-estate legal assistant. The same loan is kept by several teams. Confirming one fact, such as when a payout was received, can take a message to Payments, a look at the loan system, and a decision from someone with authority to set the settlement date. That is how cross-team checking happens in practice; it is my observation, not a measurement.

    From public sources

    Upstream inputs cause downstream errors

    The Auditor General of Canada's 2017 report on Phoenix pay problems found departments processing a large share of pay requests retroactively, which fed the backlog of pay corrections. An EY payroll survey (commissioned by a payroll vendor) reported errors in about one payroll in five, with missing or wrong time punches among the most frequent. Public lender guides, the Canadian Bar Association's note on relying on payout statements, an OBSI case on a mishandled mortgage transfer and the FCAC pages on discharge and prepayment penalties shaped the mortgage cases.

    What these sources do and do not show. They helped me confirm the problem is real and common. They do not validate Tickmark. I have not measured time saved, fewer round trips or fewer errors with real users, and each bank's own rules for holds, per diem and waivers still need checking with that bank.

    What I chose to build

    What Tickmark already did · 27 Oct – 2 Nov

    Find the difference

    Upload an HRIS file and payroll register, or a bank statement and the ledger. Run the checks, flag what differs, record each review with a reason, and export it. A stress test plants known errors to show what the rules miss.

    The next choice · 3 Nov

    Explain it, and get it answered

    Flagging was not the hard part; deciding was. So from each exception the reviewer can go back to the source evidence, see which sources are independent and which copy each other, send one clear question to a team or a named person, and re-run the checks when the answer arrives.

    • Extend reconciliation, don't replace it.Payroll and cash keep their own matching and rules; the new layer starts where they flag something.
    • Trace to the source.Two records that agree because one copied the other are one source, not two.
    • Keep urgency apart from confidence.A closing tomorrow is urgent however sure the reading is; a confident reading is not a reason to rush.
    • A person confirms what the data can't.A settlement date, a waiver, a release scope: the rule pauses only the step that depends on it and names who can decide.

    Walk through a store owner’s payouts Try the mortgage guided demo

    Out of scope on purpose: sending payments, statement requests, portal tracking and land registration. Those stay in the systems that run them; the demo shows their states, marked as simulated.

    What already exists

    Checked 4 Nov 2026, from public pages. A payment platform's own payouts page already breaks each payout into charges, refunds, adjustments and reserves, and exports the transactions behind it (Shopify Help: payout details, reserves). Accounting apps such as A2X post each payout to the books so it matches the bank deposit.

    So the numbers are not the gap. What I want to test is the step after them: a plain answer to why a payout differs, which item needs a decision and by when, and a possible reason kept apart from a confirmed one. Some of my demo cases, such as a summary that misses a deduction, are less likely where adjustments are already itemised; a reserve's release date, fees against the plan, and a duplicate refund are the ones worth putting in front of store owners first.

    What I left out

    • Team workflow for a one-person store.A store owner does not claim, assign or escalate. The owner view has none of it: five payouts, how each adds up, and what to do. The team version stays for stores with a bookkeeper.
    • A real model.Drafts, extraction and the store wording come from fixed rules. With money involved, every sentence had to trace to a record before a model writes any of them.
    • Natural-language questions.Not until the words are defined: an expired statement, funds received, a shortfall, overdue, high risk. A query tool without those definitions would answer confidently and wrongly.
    • Real connections.No processor, bank or servicing system, and no real customer data. Disputes, payments and emails are shown as states, marked as simulated.

    How I would know it works

    None of this is measured yet. This is the plan.

    • Time to a reason.Give five people the five store payouts and time each one, from opening it to saying why it differs and what they would do.
    • Wrong reasons.Count the conclusions that are wrong. A wrong reason given with confidence is worse than no answer, so this matters more than speed.
    • Possible taken as confirmed.Afterwards, ask whether the possible reason was confirmed. If people say yes before reading the source, the page is failing at its main job.
    • With real data, later.The share of payouts that tie with no manual step, and disputes answered before their deadline.

    9. 4 Nov 2026
      A store owner's view.

    10. 5 Nov 2026
      Search across every case.

      A support call starts with a payout ID, a name or an amount, not a queue. One box above every workbench page, and ⌘K, now search all 32 cases: IDs in part (1028), amounts by value ($6,850 or 6850), names, evidence and the store owner's wording, with one typo forgiven on longer words and a few plain words mapped to the system's ("twice" finds duplicates, "short" finds "less"). Every word has to match; owner:, in: and is:open, mine or overdue narrow it. Each result says which field matched. Evidence the person acting cannot open is not searched, so search never names what the case would hide. The store owner view has no search box: the owner has five payouts, not 32 cases.

      Verified: 54 automated tests, seven of them on search; checked at desktop and phone width. Not yet: anyone searching for something real; the synonyms are my guesses until then.

    11. 5 Nov 2026

    Three rules

    1. Rules calculate.Amounts, dates, differences and every rule decision stay in deterministic code that can be tested.
    2. Evidence stays attached.Each exception carries the rows and fields that raised it. Explanations cite only fields that exist. In this build they come from deterministic code; a model may draft them later, under the same rule.
    3. People release.High-severity exceptions block release until a person decides. An override needs a written reason, kept in the log.

    Where the data comes from

    Real

    SEC EDGAR XBRL

    Latest 10-K for ADP, Paychex, Workday, Intuit, Apple, Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase and Walmart. Walmart does not tag total liabilities, so the balance-sheet identity cannot be tested from tags alone. Paychex (+16.9%) and Microsoft (+17.8%) cross the 15% revenue threshold.

    Real

    Bank of Canada

    Daily EUR/CAD rates revalue a EUR 250,000 account into JE-1037. 30 September had no published rate, so the September close uses 29 September.

    Real

    FinQA

    1,147 questions on real filings (MIT licence, Chen et al., 2021). The grader reproduces all 1,108 executable gold programs; 39 need table operations. One gold label calls an 82/18 asset allocation a debt-to-equity ratio.

    Synthetic

    Close, payroll, bank lines

    Shaped like NetSuite, Ramp, Numeric, Snowflake, HRIS and payroll exports. The rules run on them in your browser; nothing is a real company or person.

    A letter from Martin

    Tickmark started as an experiment: could finance checks show their work? The rules answered that part. What they could not answer was the question I meet at work, when the money is in the bank and the loan still shows a balance: who knows which date applies, and how fast can I find out?

    So the project now rests on one rule: amounts, dates and approvals stay in code and with people. In this build, reading documents and drafting the question are done by deterministic code that stands in for a model. A model could take that work on later, because it is reversible and it is where reviewers lose time. It would not decide.

    Writing cases to break my own rules taught me more than building them did: 66 of my own cases scored 100%, and eight hostile ones dropped recall to 92.3%. The stress test did the same again, catching six real gaps on its first run.

    What is missing for production: real servicing, payments, HRIS and ledger connections, single sign-on and real permissions, sending through the bank's own channels, and results from real reviewers. Whether this saves time or prevents errors is still a question I have to test.
